Look at the age of the world and its miserliness, that thou mayest not be deceived by its youthful appearance.

In the hand of this aged one who looks young, thou seest a bunch of roses, and it is fire.

The spring is a mirage, do not be deceived by it. This Qebleh is a cross, do not worship it.

Thy portion is not even a thorn from all these roses. If they all exist, thou art not.

When thou art cut off from all thy desires, thou wilt take away from this house only what thou didst bring into it.

When they carry their baggage on the sea of the day of resurrection, the poor will reach safety.

No matter whether thou wilt save thy capital or lose it, they will take away that which they have given thee.

This world is a market place, because it gives one thing and instantly takes away another.

Although one is a silkworm, the other is a moth.

Make a candle of thy gold, that like a lamp thou mayest gain by thine own wick.

Destroy thy body and say to the nine orifices: “Be not.” Cast away thy gold and say to pure gold: “Be not.”

Put the foot of thy generosity on gold, not thy hand, that they may not call thee a worshipper of gold like a rose.

That gold which is not minted to one's own desire, is only arsenic.

If thou lovest gold for its colour, the tail of the peacock also has that colour.

The kings who take away gold by means of steel, are more blacksmiths than kings.

Though the will of Aaron made of it a crown, it sank into a well because of that mistake.

It is thy load so long as it is on thy head; it becomes thy steed, when thou treadest it under thy feet.

If to give gold is to give life, not to take it is better than that gift.

To take it gives thee greed of the world; to spend it gives thee ease of life.

Is it not better not to take it, than to take it and spend it?

When thou storest gold, it is like rich food which gives thee bile; if thou spendest it, it is like a fruit which takes away bile.

Gold which is mined in the East, is called western by the ignorant.

The West and its people are the enemies of generosity; the East and its people are enlightened by generosity.

Whatever the eastern morning gives, the western night borrows.

Gold is the ruler of the life of all mines; the feather is the power of the wings of all birds.

Though that Roman gold which, in the balance of love, is true by the stone of Damascus

And is shining and beautiful, throw dust on it, because it is deceitful.

Whose hat has this thief not carried? And who has not been misled by the wiles of this demon?
