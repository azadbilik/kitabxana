A traveller, one of the spiritual guides, was on a journey; with him were a thousand disciples.

The sage in that desert broke wind loudly and gave it as a trust to the trustees of the dust.

Every one of them left him, until they had all departed but one.

The sage said to him: “What happened, that they all departed, and thou didst remain?”

The disciple said: “Oh thou, who livest in my heart, the crown of my head is the dust of the sole of thy feet.

“I did not come by a wind at first, that the same wind should blow me back.”

He who expects a gift, will go when he has received it. He who comes by a wind, will go by a wind.

Dust travels quickly and settles quickly, therefore it cannot establish itself in one place.

The mountain comes slowly into being, therefore it is so firm.

The way of the world is to divulge secrets. The work of patience is to bear burdens.

Bear the load of piety, if thou art not lustful. Do not bear the burden of nature, if thou art not an ass.

So long as the proclamation of thy piety was not untrue, thine eyes wept on account of it, but it was not stained.

Piety in the gold-embroidered robe of the king is the story of the basket and Solomon.

The candle which scatters gold every night, is an ascetic hidden under a cloak.

Piety is a stranger in the wine tavern. Treasure is precious in a ruined place.

The piety of Nezāmi, which is a beautiful garment, shelters under the banner embroidered with gold.
