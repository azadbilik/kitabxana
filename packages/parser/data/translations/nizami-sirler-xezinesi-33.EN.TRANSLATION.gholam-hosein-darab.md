One day, Feridun went out sight-seeing with some of his friends.

When he arrived at the hunting ground, he saw a little gazelle which attracted him.

Its neck and its ears disarmed enmity; its eyes and its buttocks interceded for it.

Attracting the king’s eye, it seemed as though it had sprung from the king’s vision.

The king was so much attracted to that quarry, that he was completely enslaved by it.

In the pursuit, his horse became as hot as his heart; he made the arch of his bow as soft as the belly of the gazelle.

Notwithstanding his efforts, the arrow missed it; even at that pace the horse did not reach its dust.

Feridun said to his arrow: “Where is thy revengeful feather?” and to his horse: “Where is thy swift gallop?”

“Ye are not this time what ye both were before; ye are small compared with that little grass-eater.”

The arrow spoke: “Oh Lord, this poor wretch is the object of thy gaze.

“It runs under the protection of thy armour; who can strike upon thy armour with an arrow?

“It is not fitting that the object of the gaze of the great should be touched except by the hand of the minstrel.”

Seek the brand of the great, oh wise man, that thou mayest be great through their brand.

Service is the attribute of humanity; to serve is the glory of mankind.

In the eyes of the spiritually minded, there is no service more acceptable than fidelity.

Lay the hand of faithfulness on the girdle of fidelity; strive not to break faith.

The serpent guarding the treasure is not poor; yet it is but a girdle from head to tail.

Heaven is the crown of the head, because the whole of it is a girdle for service.

Whoso holds the reins of virtue, is girt for the road of service.

The candle which became the ruler of light, gained this lordship through the service of the bee.

Arise, oh Nezāmi, because thou hast not girded thy loins. What effort hast thou made to serve?
