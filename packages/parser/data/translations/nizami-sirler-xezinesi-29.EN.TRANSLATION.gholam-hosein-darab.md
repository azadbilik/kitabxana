Somewhere in Syria lived an old man who, like a fairy, shunned mankind.

He wove his shirt of grass. He made bricks and earned a livelihood thus.

When the warriors laid down their arms, they made those bricks their shield in the grave.

He who had only those bricks to cover his grave, even though he were a sinner, was not punished.

One day the old man was hard at work at this calling.

By chance there came to him a handsome youth,

Saying: “What humiliation and degradation is this? Brickmaking is work for an ass.

“Arise, and do not strike thy sword upon the shield of the dust. They will not grudge thee a loaf of bread.

“Throw this mould into the fire; make thy bricks with some other mould.

“How long wilt thou painfully dig clods from the earth? Why dost thou spend thy time on clay and water?

“Remember that thou art aged; leave the work of youth to the young.”

The old man said: “Do not be foolish; do not interfere. Do not consider thyself important.

“The aged make bricks; captives carry loads.

“I have put my hand to this profession of mine, that I might not one day have to stretch out my hand to thee.

“I beg for treasure from none. I eat my daily bread through the work of mine own hands.

“Do not trouble me for this my daily bread. If this is not so, may it be forbidden me.”

The young man went away weeping, because of the reproachful words of the old man.

This old man was experienced, and his work was acceptable.

How long, oh Nezāmi, wilt thou knock at the door of this world? Arise, and knock at the door of religion, if thou wishest to knock.
