Oh heaven, how much longer wilt thou revolve so slowly? Oh earth, how much longer wilt thou endure this tyranny contentedly?

After each night comes the dawn; the end of every burden is to be unloaded.

Fear is cast into the spheres of the earth. “The hour of the earthquake is a supreme thing.”

The madness of the raging earth broke the link of the chain of heaven.

The wind broke up the distracted earth; the mad earth will break the chain.

With whom did the earth make a wager to loosen the girdle of heaven?

The night lost its colour and the dawn its perfume. Heaven ceased to be a polo-stick, and earth escaped from being a ball.

The earth knocks at the door of the high heaven, and the girded heaven lies in ambush.

The cataclysm of heaven emerged from the ambush and disintegrated the earth limb by limb.

The old heaven will rend its robe and will break the string of the clay bead.

Heaven will come down and will unbend; the earth will go swinging up.

Both heaven and earth will be freed from our troubles; our dust will be cleansed from both roads.

The heavens will cease to work for thee; the earth will be immune from thy guile.

The stars and the sky were ashamed. How much longer should they worship a handful of dust?

The round heaven is like a serpent; in the end the serpent will eat dust.

Oh ye, who have grieved the earth, which of you is outside this earth?

Why is the dust in this vessel of grief? Why is the colour of its vessel the blue of mourning?

If ye cannot lie in ambush and throw out this clay from this vessel,

Wash your skirt clean seven times with the water and dust of this smoking vessel.

The robe of the stars was lifted from the heavens; the line of destruction was drawn on the world.

A sudden disaster will fall upon the earth from the swiftly revolving heaven.

The signs which portend upheaval, are shown by the agitation of the heavens.

The head jerks when it is broken; this jerk is in this world to-day.

This black oyster is thine enemy; its eyes are full of pearls, but its heart full of whales.

This is not the pearl of the sea, and this is not the jewel of the mine of vision.

He who saw it was saddened and lost his sight like a dragon looking at an emerald;

Therefore he has no eyesight; he has a thousand eyes but no sight.

Thou hast not chosen the road of non-existence, because thou hast seen it with the eyes of others.

Use thine own feet; one cannot walk with the feet of others.

Even though Bahrām is lifted up as high as heaven by gold and power, his portion is the grave.

One cannot close the door of this road; one cannot raise the head above this roof.

Remain in this prison which has its doors and shutters closed, as if thou wert stricken with typhus.

How long wilt thou discuss and remember heaven? May the humble dust be upon its proud head.

Do not be grieved by the sky and its milky way; do not consider the straw carrier worth a barley-corn.

Soar above this revolving dome, that thou mayest be free from the circle of the narrow compass.

Imagination which is the thinnest thread, has turned back ashamed from this narrow road.

See the helplessness of shame-faced imagination; look carefully at this narrow road.

On this tight-rope do not cling to a hair; otherwise come thou out like a hair from the dough.

Since thou mayest obtain better profit than this, it would be unwise to stay here.

Since this heap of dust is unfaithful, it is not wise to turn to it.

Every crown is a sign of humiliation; every belt is stained with a hundred slaveries.

Every merit bears the ridicule of a city. In every sweetmeat is the sting of poison.

The fire of dawn in this kitchen is half a spark from the flames of hell.

The moon whose body is the candle of heaven, gets its oil from begging at the door of the sun.

The cloud which gives life to that which is withered, is itself the humour of depression.

The ship knows what great loss water, which is the ease of life, can bring.

This world is a house full of faults; thou dost not look at its defects.

Thou art blind to thine own faults and hast become a mirror for the faults of others.

Do not show faults like a mirror, that thou mayest not be dimmed by a breath.

Either cast out merit by thine absence; or break the mirror of thine own fault.

Close thine eyes to the faults of others; look at thine own picture and find the faults there.

There is virtue and vice in everything; do not look for faults, and thou wilt find merit.

Can one not see at night by lamp light? Can one see the crow only in the light of the day?

How can it be fitting to find fault with the feet of the peacock, when it has golden feathers?

The crow whose body is all black, has white eyes; look and thou wilt see.
