O thou, whose body is purer than the pure soul! Thy soul is nourished by May my soul be sacrificed to thee.

Thou art the centre of the circle of mercy; thou art the destroyer of the dot of zahmat.

Thou art the moon of the Arabian travellers; thou art the king of the kings of Irān.

They seek the path by thee, and thou dost not guide. Thou art thyself the lord of the village; yet thou art not in the village.

When the great like thee go on a journey, they do not themselves alone consume the provision for all.

What hast thou brought back for us from that table at which thou hast partaken of fresh dates?

Open thy mouth, that all may partake of sweetmeats and take fresh dates from the water of thy mouth.

Oh thou, whose dark tresses are the day of salvation, the fire of the love of thee is the water of life.

Reason is distracted for love of thy face; thy tresses are the chains of the maniacs.

The heavens are a slave to thy girdle. The dawn, compared with the sun of thy face, is but a smile.

The impure were cleansed of vice by thee. The navel of the earth obtained its musk-bladder from thee.

That land is loaded with musk by thy musk-scented dust.

Thy dust is better than the wind of Solomon; how should I compare it with paradise? It is better than the Guardian Angel of paradise.

The Ka’beh, the altar of thy devotions, thirsts for a draught of the rose water of thine elixir.

The world holds thy crown and thy throne; thy throne is the earth, and thy crown is the heavens.

Thou, who art the light of the great, hast no shadow; thou art thyself the shadow of the light of God.

The four standards are the pillars of Islām; the five prayers are the insignia of empire.

The dust of the abject has become a garden through thee. The eyes of strangers have their sight from thee.

Since thy foot has trodden in the dark night upon the crown of the revolving heaven,

Thou hast filled its lap with gold and pearls and made the buttons of its robe of small gold bricks.

In the oyster shell of the dawn, the morning breeze mixes the ambergris of thy perfume with the hands of purity,

Therefore the army of ambergris lowered its standard wherever the morning breezes blew.

If thou gavest the perfume which thy ringlets diffuse for the price of the two worlds, thou wouldst give it cheaply.

The Tree of Heaven is a ribbon decorating thy robe; the heavenly Throne is an usher in thy court.

When the light of thy soul shines in the morning, that Throne will be but a mote in that sunshine.

If from the Dawn of Eternity a mirror had not reflected thy light, how could thy light have fallen upon the dust of the earth?

Oh thou, who art both worlds, why art thou under the earth? Thou art not an earthly treasure; why art thou bound to the dust?

It is necessary to entrust the treasure to the dust, so long as thou art in the dust, oh pure treasure.

Thy poverty is a ruin sufficient for thy treasure. Thy shadow is a moth sufficient for thy candle.

The curved heaven is the target of thy prayers, the wheel of its Aquarius is the rope of thy well.

These two revolving black and white attendants are messengers on the road amongst thy train.

Reason seeks a cure; thou art its physician. The moon is the traveller; thou art her object of wonder.

Arise, turn the night of the expectant into day, and light the poetic mind of Nezāmi with joy.
