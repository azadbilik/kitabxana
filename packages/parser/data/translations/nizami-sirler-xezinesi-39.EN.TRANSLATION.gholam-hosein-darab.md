The feet of Christ, walking on the earth, passed across a small market place.

He saw a wolf dog which had fallen by the cross roads; its life had gone out of its body.

A crowd like carrion-eating vultures was gazing at that carcass.

One said: “The fear of this brings darkness to the mind, as breath blows out the lamp.”

Another said: “That is not all; it blinds the eyes and oppresses the heart.”

Everyone sang on this note and made cruel allusions to the carcass.

When the turn of Jesus came to speak, he discarded faults and found virtue.

He said: “The picture remaining of its body shows that pearls are not so white as its teeth.”

And the others, through fear and hope, whitened their teeth with that burnt oyster shell.

Do not seek the faults of others and thine own merit; turn thine eyes down to thyself.

On the day when thou takest the mirror into thine own hand, be modest; do not worship thyself.

Do not adorn thyself like the spring, that time may not destroy thee.

The garment of thy faults is woven very thin, therefore they have put thee behind nine curtains.

What is in this ring which, if thou wilt look at it closely, is not a badge of servitude?

If thou art not a dog, do not wear even the collar of the Pleiades. If thou art not an ass, do not carry even the load of the Messiah.

What is heaven but an old widow? What is earth but a rotten fruit?

The whole world from its beginning to its end, is not worth a grain of barley, because it is transient.

Do not grieve for the world, oh master; arise, if thou grievest, pour out the wine of happiness for Nezāmi.
