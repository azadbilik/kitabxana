How quickly the sweet day of life reached its evening. Dust and wind came together and fire and water.

Morning has come, why art thou drunken with sleep? The sun has passed the top of the wall.

Abandon this pursuit of conquest. Do not pretend to youth, old age is here.

That heart which was wounded by grief, is dry; it has no longer its former savour.

Reason is darkened, and judgment is destroyed; the hand is blistered, and the foot is weary.

Earth desires to bestow favours upon thee; withdraw thy foot; it is the time of rest.

In this purity and pollution, there is no better comfort than ease.

The source of thy moonlight has grown cold. Thy cheeks which were fresh like tulips, are yellow.

Black and white have fought with every part of thy body, from head to foot.

Time which is thy night and day, teaches thee during thy youth.

There were many younger than thee in the world who desired not to grow old.

The wind of autumn carried away the petals of his rose; age came and carried off his youth.

They have not found fault with youth. There is a saying: “Old age and a hundred faults.”

Even though the kingdom be the kingdom of Jamshid, white hair is the sign of despair.

White hair brings the message of death; the bent back brings greetings from death.

Who possesses the empire of youth and beauty? It is not mine, oh Lord, tell me whose it is.

Youth has passed in negligence. It is lamentable, lament therefore.

If the lost beloved is a Joseph, the loss is grievous.

Thou knowest not the value of youth; thou wilt not know it, until thou art old.

The young tree is the pride of the garden; when it is old, the gardener cuts it down.

Although youth is itself all fire, age is bitter, and youth is sweet.

The new shoot bears the early rose; the dry wood is for the fire.

Black hair is the beauty of the head. The black stone is the touchstone for gold.

The days of youth come to an end. Awake, night has passed, and morning has come, awake.

When camphor subdued the fire of thy nature, it changed the nature of thy musk into that of camphor.

When the weather is cold for a time, the black cloud brings the white snow.

Bleaching and dyeing go together. The sun and the Messiah live in the same sphere.

The quality of water is to bleach; the function of the moon is to colour.

This blue dome loves changing colour, therefore Jesus practised the dyer’s art.

Since the air has no colour, it has no weight.

Do not be party-coloured like day and night; do not have a white face and black cheeks.

So long as thou seekest hypocrisy, thou bearest the brand of ignorance and darkness.

On the mountain, the leopard is wounded through the duality of his colour.

So long as, like the young trees, thou clothest thyself at times in silk and at times in rags,

Through this contradictory nature thou hast an armour: in the summer thou hast a hundred warm garments, and in the winter thou art naked.

Eat and drink that which thou canst obtain all the year round like the lion and the leopard.

So long as thou hast a loaf of bread and a draught of water, do not stretch out thy hand to every bowl.

If thou hast not enough bread to satisfy thy hunger, who can keep from thee water and herbs?

It is better to eat grass, like the ass of Jesus, than to beg bread from others.

The fire of the dust of the curve of this whirlwind will not give bread, unless it takes away man’s honour.

If in this tower of the imprisoned dead, the fire of the spiritually enlightened gave no heat,

Why should a wolf be the Joseph of its soul? And why should the lion-hearted be the cat begging at its table?

Do not grind the grain of thy heart like barley and wheat for the sake of a handful of false wheat.

Make a relish, like water, of thine own breast for thy bread, and, like fire, roast the flesh of thine own heart.

Eat dust rather than eat the bread of the miser. Thou art not dust, do not suffer the wound of the base.

Pierce thy heart and hand with the thorn of toil. Do not seek comfort; put thy hand to work.

It is good that thou shouldst work, so that thou mayest not beg from others.
