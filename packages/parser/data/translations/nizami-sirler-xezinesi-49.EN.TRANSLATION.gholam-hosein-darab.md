I heard a story that in far-off Marv there was a young prince, like a cypress.

He was troubled on account of the nobles of his country. The country loved him as did fortune.

The aged were fighting his youth. He was in peril from that approaching danger.

One night he fell asleep full of anxiety on account of that rebellion. In that condition he saw a sage in a dream, who said to him:

“Oh new moon, destroy the old tower; oh fresh rose, cut off the old branch,

“That the empire may be confirmed to thee and thy life become more pleasant than thy nature.”

When the king awoke from his deep sleep, he destroyed those persons.

He built up anew and destroyed the old. The empire was confirmed anew to that young king.

The destroyer of the empire should be humbled. A treacherous army is better dispersed.

The new branch will not grow from the trunk of the cypress, unless the old branch is cut off.

Unless the water is diverted from the stream, the plane tree will not unfold its leaves.

Unless the passage to the spring be clear, water will not be born from the heart and the eye of the clay.

With thee, but outside thee, is one who feeds thy heart. He gives good counsel to thine ears.

Draw that sword from its scabbard for an instant. How long wilt thou keep it sheathed, oh thou, its enemy?

That breath comes not from the casket of this dust. Thou dost not give that pure companion his due.

Sacrifice everything to such a being and be wholly entitled to the name of generosity.

The rich who attained to honour and wealth, found the eternal empire through generosity.

The seed of generosity gives sure harvest. When it ripens, it gives a provision for the day of judgment.

Oh God, give to Nezāmi pure gold from the treasure of thy generosity.
