A religious man was attracted to evil; he chose the street of the wine sellers.

He put wine to his lips and wept bitterly, saying: “Oh wretch that I am, what is my remedy?

“The bird of desire has rested in my heart, it made the beads of my rosary into a snare.

“Mecca was the thief of my time; my original home was the wine tavern.

“My star was evil, and I suffered misfortune; I was destined for the street of the wine-drinkers.

“The eyes of modesty are hidden through my fault. Wine is drunk in the streets of the wine-sellers through my fault.

“May the world be delivered from me, the forsaken one; may my dust be far from my skirt.

“If it were not fate, why should I be an idolater? How could a pious man frequent taverns?”

Since he was looked on favourably by the spiritually minded, a youth who was in that circle, said:

“Keep fate out of this affair; to Providence a hundred thousand like thee are not worth a barley-corn.

“Come to the door of repentance and wash away thy sin; then speak in this way.”

When thou goest, they receive thee, forgiving thy sins; otherwise they will come themselves and take thee captive.

It is enough, give up grazing on the grass of the earth; heaven should be thy green sugar-cane.

That sleep may not overcome thee, avoid it. Make a little provision for the time of non-existence.

It is unfitting that the eyes should weep blood for the same sleep in life and death.

When religion saw thee in this deep sleep, she hid her face behind the veil.

Arise Nezāmi, because the king has mounted. Why art thou fettered here at midnight?
