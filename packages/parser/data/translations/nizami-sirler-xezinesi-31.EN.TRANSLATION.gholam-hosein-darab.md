There was a hunter who had wonderfully sharp sight. He travelled far and wide in the desert.

He had such a courageous dog that, when it ran, it outpaced the sun.

The rhinoceros went in terror of its strength; the wild ass, of its devouring teeth.

On his travels, it was his companion and friend; it would work for him night and day.

Love burnt in his heart for it; it was his guardian by night and provided his daily bread.

That lion-dog was lost to the brave man; the man feared that the dog had been destroyed.

He said: “In this perilous journey, the price of a dog’s foot is a lion’s head.”

Although this grief broke his heart, yet he bore it with patience.

He was patient beyond bearing; each grain of his patience earned him a dram of profit.

A fox came to him from a distance, mocking him, and said: “Do not be patient, oh impatient one;

“I hear that swift runner is dead. May your life be long, though the dog has disappeared.

“Last night when he left you to hunt, he ran swiftly and vanished.

“This which the dog caught for thee to-day, must suffice thee for two months, oh brave man.

“Arise and make roast meat of thy heart, eat the kernel and give the husk to the poor.

“Formerly thou hadst rich food; now thou wilt no longer eat fat foxes.

“Thou art now without the fat of our limbs, thy nature is free from our bile.

“Thou art separated from him; what fidelity is this? Thou dost not grieve; what love is this?”

The hunter said to the fox: “The night is still pregnant; this one day’s grief is mine.

“I rejoice that in this narrow house neither happiness nor sorrow will last.

“All this mastery and servitude exist in this revolving sphere.

“The stars and the heavens revolve; ease and toil will pass away.

“I am happy that my heart is grieved, since grief is the cause of happiness.

“My wolf suffered the fate of Joseph. I am not a wolf; I will not rend my garment.

“If they have taken it from me, oh deceitful one, they will bring it back with a prey like thee.”

He was still speaking when dust arose, and the dog appeared through the veil of dust.

It came and ran round and round him; it caught hold of the fur of the fox with its teeth.

The dog said: “Excuse me that I came late; the fox knows that I came like a lion.

“My return enhanced thy faith; the helplessness of the fox is an assurance to thee.”

His affairs will end happily who is drawn by assurance to the divine purpose.

Seek the path of assurance in everything; nothing is more blessed than this stage of the road.

The foot becomes the head on the path of assurance; the stone becomes gold in the eye of assurance.

If thy footsteps are firm in assurance, thou wilt get dust from the sea and moisture from fire.

He who united assurance to trust, wrote on generosity: “God will give sustenance.”

He was no fly on another man’s food; he accepted whatever came to him.

Thy sustenance will not be taken from thee; do the work of God and be not anxious for thy daily bread.

Go to His door, because He is better than they. Ask thy sustenance of Him, because He gives thy daily bread.

No one of us who begged at that door, returned empty-handed.

Those who have assurance, are a different race; we are the feet, and they are the head.

When they spread their prayer carpet on the water, they change the pure wine into honey.

Since life is not assured to thee for a day, why shouldst thou lay in a provision for a hundred years?

When they created us, they apportioned our daily bread to us in eternity.

Since they have sent thee thy daily bread from thence, thou consumest here only thy portion.

Although many have striven in this world, whoever ate more than his portion?

Strive to excel in faith, because this is thy covenant; sustenance and wealth are not increased by effort.

It requires thy effort and God’s grace to make thee more precious than the whole world.

The effort of Nezāmi was not strenuous; divine favour prospered him.
