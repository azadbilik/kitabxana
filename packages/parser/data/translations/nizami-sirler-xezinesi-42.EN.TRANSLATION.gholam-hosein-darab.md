Rise and say farewell to the world; release thy skirt from this snare.

Prepare a better kingdom than this; open a better shop than this.

When thy heart and eye finish their journey, they should groan and shed tears,

So that, by moistening this clay, thou mayest boast of mastery over thine own heart.

If thou art a camel, dance on the journey; otherwise do not throw wine-skins under the feet of elephants.

Since thou hast none whom thou canst trust, only non-existence can be thy confidant.

The boon companions and friends have gone on; with whom art thou sitting, since thy friends have gone?

Although thy poetic talent may attract thee with subtle imagery, how can it alone be a companion to thee?

It is better that a pious heart should seek the light of purity in this black dust.

Before the stage of disruption is reached on the road, discard thy possessions.

Cast away thy baggage, because thou movest slowly, so that, having a light burden, thou mayest quickly reach thy destination.

Come to heaven, if thou seekest the Heart. What couldst thou gain in this house?

Since thou art entangled in this snare, tear it, that thou mayest escape onto the road,

Because this circle which is like a Mim, will not let thee out, unless it is broken in half.

Do not be the butt of this chequered dome; do not obey the orders of this circle.

If thou art free from the orders of day and night, thou wilt be outside the circle of this world.

Until thou canst make thy footsteps firm, do not step forward in search of any work.

Prepare a way of escape from any work, before thou enterest on it.

It is wise to look at the road, so that one may avoid falling into a pit.

Make an opening in this house of deluge, that thou mayest have opportunity to escape.

A cunning fox scented a dog; he made two exits to his earth,

But was unaware that the smoke of this deceitful heaven would close the exits.

What is this happiness which pleases thee? Thou art unaware that thou art ignorant of thyself.

It was destined that thou shouldst arrive in and depart from this world in sorrow.

If now thou breakest the divine covenant, how can thy soul be relieved of its burden of responsibility?

Walk in the way that the soul has shown thee; throw away both worlds, because thou hast seen the world.

Do not look down, that thou mayest not fear the depth; do not look back, that thou mayest not fear the shadow.

Take a provision of religion, because there are few habitations on the road; bring the water of thy tears, because the road is waterless.

Give the pure pearl into the custody of the oyster shell. Free thyself and set thy dust free.

The revolving heaven has killed many a friend such as thee; it has slain many more powerful than thee.

Make a supreme effort in this duel, so that some time thou mayest throw it to the ground.

There is no need to fear the shield and sword of him who is not skilled in this art.

Do not call it a snake, because this twisted rope is naught against the attraction of thy love.

How can one grieve for this glass which can be broken by the wind?

The generous ones who have extinguished the fire of gold, have conquered their enemies by a sweetmeat.

So long as thou canst; through thine enlightened heart, like the sun, kill thine enemy with a rose.
