One day, when Solomon was at liberty, his breath blew out a candle.

He transferred his court to the country. He set up his throne on this turquoise realm.

He saw an old farmer in such a state on that arid plain, that his heart was broken.

He had taken corn from his house and had cast it into the granary of divine generosity.

He was sowing seed in all directions. An ear of corn grew from each of his grains.

When he opened his bag of corn, he loosed the tongues of the birds of Solomon.

One said: “Oh old man, be content, consume that which thou hast.

“Thou art not a snare, do not scatter grain; do not contend vainly with us.

“Thou hast no spade, do not scratch the surface of the desert. Thou dost not find water, do not sow the seed of the farmer.

“What harvest did we obtain, who cultivated a well irrigated land,

“That thou shouldst produce anything in this parched waterless burning field?”

The old man said: “Do not take offence at the answer; I am independent of the produce of earth and water.

“Water and drought are no concern of mine. I sow the seed, but God helps the growth.

“My water is the sweat of my body; my spade the tips of my fingers.

“I have no need for empire or lands. As long as I live, this is enough for me.

“He who gives good tidings through me, gives me seven hundred grains for each grain that I sow.”

Do not sow grain in company with Satan, so that every seed may produce seven hundred grains.

One must first sow good seed, that the ear of corn may ripen well.

Every created form has been endowed with a garment fitted to his stature.

Not every donkey can carry the garments of Christ. Not every one can be trusted with the secrets of the empire.

A rhinoceros can eat the neck of an elephant; an ant cannot consume more than the leg of a locust.

The sea is calm in spite of a hundred rivers; a spadeful makes the brook noisy.

In this circle of lapis lazuli, the position of every man accords with his worth.

It requires a fortunate patient man not to get tired of his burden.

Not every soul is worthy of grace; not every womb is pregnant with divine secrets.

I will not repeat it, because it shows inexperience: the work of Nezāmi is to carry a load.
