When Alef was drawn on the First Tablet, it was fixed as a beading to the door of Ahmad.

When Alef gave the empire to the ring of Hā, it gave that same Hā a collar from Dāl and a belt from Mim,

Therefore it received from Mim and Dāl the circle of the empire and the line of perfection.

Under this dome of turquoise bricks, there grew a fresh tangerine tree from the garden of Paradise.

It is the nature of a tangerine tree that in the world it should first bring forth fruit and then blossom.

When “I was a Prophet” carried the banner forward, it entrusted the completion of prophetship to Mohammad.

The moon is the bezel of emerald; its gem is the seal of Mohammad.

The ear of the world carries the Mim of his bondage; the two worlds themselves submit to him.

He is the great Surveyor, and the Anointed is his slave; they are both called messengers of good tidings.

He is illiterate, but speaks with eloquence from the Alef of Adam to the Mim of the Messiah.

He is straight like Alef in his promise and fidelity. He is the beginning and the end of the prophets.

He is the most brilliant centre of the compass of “Be thou,” the centre of the all inclusive circle of speech.

The whole world of literature is but an empty sound compared with his speech, and the heavens are a girdle for his waist.

Although he has no pride, yet he did not bow down to the world.

The veiled ones of heaven are the doorkeepers of his sanctuary. Chastity itself is nurtured by him.

His dust cleanses sin from the eyes. His departure wrests revenue from Mecca.

His silence is heart-ravishing speech; his friendship is a virtue which destroys faults.

To quell the disturbance by him is the heart’s delight, also there can be no escape from the madness of his love.

He was the leader of all and the beginning of all good. He was the great fixed star, but moved swiftly.

In his heart the divine candle was lit; he learnt the lesson of eternity from the beginning to the end.

The source of the sun, dependent on him, is but half the crescent moon of the night of his Ascent to Heaven.

He was the crowned monarch of the Night of Ascension; he enthroned kings with belts and crowns.

He gave ease to weary breath; he shod the heavenly steed which carried him by night.

The riders of speech stood motionless ready with their steeds, expecting his return.

When the chequered steed ceased to gallop, Nezāmi was appointed as its groom.
