I who in this circle which goes round the world am tied to the city like the centre of a point,

Have no opportunity to stretch my limbs in comfort. I have the shadow but not the glory of the Phœnix.

My foot is sunk deep in this mire; my hand is on the saddle-strap of heaven.

I lowered the crown of my head to beneath my feet, and I made a footstep of my knee-cap.

The mirror of my heart on my knee-cap has become the great brightness of my face.

I who occupied myself with this mirror, looked through the mirror of my physical eye

To see from which mirror light may come, or from which fire water may reach me.

When the eye of reason with just judgment quickly searched the world,

It saw, of that essence which is in the spirit, one who is the benefactor of the noble-minded,

The mighty king whose fortune is victorious, the rose tree of this turquoise-coloured garden,

A Khezr with the dignity of Alexander, of just judgment, the fixed star which determines the astronomical tables and explains the Almagest,

He who is first in the purpose of existence, and for whose sake the Verse of Purpose was revealed,

The crowned king, owner of Solomon’s signet ring, the pride of the world, King Fakhrod-Din.

His descent from David makes the name of Solomon befit his dignity.

Through him the banner of Isaac is raised aloft. If he has an enemy, it is an Esmā’ili.

He is single-hearted; he holds the six dimensions and the seven spheres; Bahrām Shāh is the centre of the nine circles.

It is he who, because of his valour in the trial of strength, assigns a grave as a portion to Bahrām-e Gur.

King of kings in his great ability, famous in the world for his great wisdom,

He sits on the throne of the Sultan and the Caliph and is the conqueror of Mesopotamia and the Abkhāz.

More learned and just than all living, most beloved and noblest of the munificent,

His religion is the heaven and his kingdom the stars. His country is the oyster shell; the dust of his threshold is the pearl.

He is the spring and the sea full of fish and of pearls, a peaceful spring and a full sea.

The sun escapes like quicksilver because of his generous hand.

The brilliant pure ruby on his belt laughs at the ruby on the belt of the sun.

The calamity of this window of lapis lazuli struck him who laid hands on him.

His bell deafens the ears of heaven; his breath breaks the mirror of the moon.

A better beginning than happiness, a better end than the milk of human kindness,

May his hand, which is the cup-bearer of the bowl of generosity, last for ever, because the bowl is everlasting.
