There is an artist behind this curtain; otherwise who could have shown these scenes on it?

Make the eye of thy heart familiar with this curtain to perceive that which comes from behind this veil.

Behind this blue curtain, he has countless instruments.

They have lit the gems of their eyes in reverence and have sewn them on the girdle of their heart’s service.

There is nothing on any point of this circle, which is not working on the line of its circumference.

These steeds which are saddled, are chosen for us.

Before the movement of this new creation, and before the new travellers and the old voices,

Had we not built the foundation of love? Had we not been nourished by love?

There are evil and good in both worlds; and they have tied both to thy saddle strap.

There is no companion for the world like thee; the bird of the world has no better grain than thee.

Leave this bird which is injurious to nature. Be above this bird like the Phœnix.

The bird, thy Messiah, has wings beneath thee and is above thee.

Either extricate its claws from the cage; or entrust thine own cage to it,

That, when it carries the baggage to its destination, it may carry thee on its protecting wings.

When thou passest beyond these spheres of dust, they cleanse thy tablet of thee.

Thou wilt transcend alteration. Thou wilt be entrusted with the divine secret.

Thou wilt walk with ease in step with the prophets; thou wilt be initiated into the divine temple.

The two stages of the two worlds are but half a stage of one breath of the Heart.

He who laid thy foundations on this clay, made the sanctuary of the soul in the temple of the Heart.

Receive the picture of acceptance from the enlightened Heart; expect naught from the black carpet of the body.

The morning breeze draws collyrium to the eye of the narcissus. Alchemy dyes the garment in copper.

What is the body? It is a handful of clay. Only the Heart counts, because the Word is in the Heart.

Be the slave of the Heart, that thou mayest be a king, the master of reason, and the ruler of the soul.

If thou seekest a heart as soft as the fur of the fox, give thy body to hardship like musk.

Oh thou, who hast no garment better than sackcloth, authority comes not from silk and brocade.

The beauty of the gazelle is in the roughness of its skin; therefore it is chosen to carry messages of love.

Musk remains in rough parchment and escapes from silk.

If thou art sugar, be content with compression; if thou art a pearl, be content with a hard shell.

Sometimes be like a ruby dawn, ending the night; sometimes, like the dawn, be the target of sighs.

Endure the burden of distress in the pitch dark night; the greater the distress, the greater the favour.

Those faithful ones who reached their destination, reached it along the path of affliction.

The gift of calamity is the happiness of the prophets, and that which gives thee happiness is calamity.

The wound of calamity is the remedy of selfishness; the bitterness of wine is the essence of its sweetness.

The dragons are the guardians of treasures, and discomfort guards the treasure of ease.

Be a cypress and be free from thy fetters. Be a candle and be happy in consuming thyself.

Toil is the remedy of complaint, and beyond every toil there is rest.

Providence will not bring trouble upon thee, unless it takes other troubles from thee.

On the journey which leads to freedom, the guardian grief is the fore-runner of happiness.
