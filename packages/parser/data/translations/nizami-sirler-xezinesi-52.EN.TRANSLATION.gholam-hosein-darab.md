Oh thou, who neglectest God and thyself, remaining in the grief of thy soul and the toil of thy body,

Be silent; the movements of the boasting spirit which is in this world, reach only to the lips.

Do not embrace the world like the arch of heaven; do not bind thyself to what is not thine.

The power of the world is greater than the power of thine arm; its weights are too heavy for thy scales.

Do not expect the strength of a mountain from a speck of dust. Do not expect to boil a cauldron on one spark.

Every loin which is girt with the belt of contentment, is freed for ever from the service of the body.

The harvest of greed is disappointment; the crown of contentment is on the head of submission.

In this defile, there are many pickpockets; he whose purse is emptiest, is most at ease.

If thou art wealthy, submit to anxiety; otherwise go, hang on the skirts of poverty.

There was a beardless man who grieved at his condition. He saw two men fighting and pulling each other’s beards.

He said: “Though my face is hairless like a tongue, I am glad to be safe from those who pull beards.”

They have deemed it advisable to take away thine ass and thy load,

So that, like Jesus, thou mayest reach the door of the Heart, and arrive at thy destination without an ass and without a load.

If thou art a believer, do not ponder on fire-worship. Strive for refinement, do not be uncouth.

This is a wave of destruction, go with less speed. Throw thy burden into the water, and save thy soul.

It is better for thee to be pithless and ruined, that thou mayest float on the water like a gourd.

There is value in eating little and sleeping little. The treasure of greatness is in destruction.

Thou art not a lover of carrion like a kite; be a crow, do not dip thy feet in blood.

If thy body is bloodless like a picture, thou wilt be saved from being eaten by vultures.

Know that blood is a heart turned to wine, a fire turned to water by shame.

That thou mayest break the power of blood, thou wilt suffer the stab of the lancet, even if thou art iron.

Do not suddenly give up the habit of eating; save by eating less.

The lion is proud, because it eats little. It is the nature of fire to consume much.

Since the day was satisfied with one loaf, it became the light of the eye of the wise.

When the night drank its morning draught too early, the excess of its blood turned it black.

Excess of food lessens reason. It makes the heart which is like sweet herbs, into the target of grief.

Thy reason is a soul, and thou art its body. Thy soul is a treasure, and thou art its talisman.

How can this treasure give thee light, until thou breakest the talisman of its door?

Dust is known to be untrustworthy. Say: “Let there be no association with the untrustworthy.”

The dust will not grieve for thee, were it to bring the whole of thy life to an end in grief; do not grieve.

Someone said to a negro: “Oh father, why this laughter? One should weep at such blackness as thine?”

He answered: “As I have no hope in this world, it is better to have a black face and to laugh.”

It is not strange to find happiness in a black face, because a black cloud gives out lightning.

Since thou hast no desire for this prison, be lightning and laugh at the whole world.

The laughter of the parrot lessens the sweetness of sugar. Loud laughter closes the mouth of the partridge.

It is better to weep than to open the mouth in untimely laughter.

To burn and to laugh like lightning, shortens life like a flame.

What is this laughter without joy like a candle? One should weep much at this laughter.

Bite thy lip in laughter, that thou mayest not laugh immoderately.

To weep much is not good for the eyes; too much laughter is not becoming.

There must be a due proportion of good and evil in the old and the young.

Rise, grieve and be happy. Sometimes one and sometimes the other is fitting.

There is a sorrowful cry in a happy heart. Within the jet of the night, there is the jewel of the day.

No one has indulged in his lusts without afterwards receiving a blow.

Every load of baggage has its own bell; every sweetmeat has its own fly.

Providence is thy wise nurse; leave thy good and evil to her care.

If she gives thee vinegar, do not ferment like syrup. She desires thy good. What knowest thou? Be silent.

The serious traveller on this road must make constant effort. It needs a Moses to be the travelling companion of the Prophet Khezr.

Thou must serve the great to reach greatness.

In time of need, the helpful friend is he who withdraws the snare, not he who drags one down to calamity.
