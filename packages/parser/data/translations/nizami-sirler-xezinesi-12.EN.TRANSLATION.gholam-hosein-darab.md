I who am the singer of this new rose, am the melodious nightingale of thy garden.

I live on the road of thy love; I ring a bell down thy street.

I have not accepted a loan from any man; I have spoken what my heart has bidden me to say.

I have created a new magic; I have made a figure from a new mould.

For a while I studied the sciences of polite learning; I embroidered the magic curtain of the dawn.

It holds the capital of poverty and of kingship; it holds the Treasury of the Divine Mysteries.

No fly has tainted its sugar; neither has its fly contaminated the sugar of any man.

Noah throws down his shield, when he reaches this sea; Khezr breaks his pitcher at this spring.

I put up this beauty to be raffled for by all the kings; thy name was the fortunate winner.

Two books came from two famous places; each was dedicated to a Bahrām Shāh.

One has minted gold from the old mine, and the other has produced pearls from the new sea.

One has raised its banner in Ghaznin, and the other has struck the Roman die on its coin.

Although the words on that coin are like gold, my golden coin is better still.

Though my baggage is less than his; yet I have a better customer.

The style is wonderful, accept it. It will not be strange, if thou shouldst favour it,

Because these words which are set in a better order than the design of a garden, are not lit by external means like a lamp.

In this world, it is more prosperous than the world itself, newer than the heaven, yet born before it.

It takes no colour from any existing sign; it does not fit any existing tongue.

None has touched the provender of speech at thy table; partake thou of it.

If it is savoury, partake of it; may it nourish thee; if not, mayest thou forget it.

On the night when thou sittest at the table of heaven, throw down a bone to me,

Because, indeed, I boast that I am thy dog; I am proud to be thy slave.

Out of all the kings who have shown me fidelity, I have desired to attach myself to thee.

My service will at last lead to some recognition, and the end of this cord will reach its place.

Though the eulogists have prostrated themselves at this enduring threshold,

They stand respectfully before Nezāmi; he is different; who are these others?

I who have remained in this, their stage, have travelled on a stage beyond them.

I made my sword from the diamond of my tongue; I beheaded all who followed me.

The sword of Nezāmi which strikes off heads, is not blunted, though it is old.

Though this dignity itself is unrivalled; yet my foot aspires to a still higher position.

The pinnacle is high; I soar above it, maybe that I shall prosper by my high spirit,

So that, perchance, by the light of thy judgment, I may place my head where thy foot rests.

I follow thee, that I may reach to heaven. If thou bearest me not, how shall I reach it?

It was my intention during these last months to renew the covenant of my prostration before the king.

Though in this circle which they have drawn, they have closed the road of my exit,

I desired so eagerly to come before thee to receive increase of favours, that my body could not contain my soul,

But when I looked again, there were lions all the way; before and behind me were daggers and swords;

Yet in this place which is encircled by swords, I read the Khotbeh to thee with a loud voice.

I have sprinkled thy threshold with the water of speech; I am the pebble which has remained in its place.

Oh sun, I am like a mote before thee; may my morning prayers be answered!

My heart has become a sea pouring out pearls to thee; the jewel of my soul has become the ornament on thy girdle.

So long as night and day exist, may thy nights be days; may the jewel of thy kingship illuminate the night.

May this house of thine be auspicious, and may that house of thine be yet more auspicious.
