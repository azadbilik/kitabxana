There was a king who tyrannised over his subjects, and he had the art of Hajjāj in his inquisitions.

Whatever the night brought forth from the day, was known to him in the morning.

One morning, someone went to the king, disclosing more secrets than the sun and the moon.

He had stored up games of the night from the moon, and had learned airs and graces from the dawn.

He said: “A certain old man secretly called thee a murderer, a tyrant, and a shedder of blood.”

The king was enraged at these words and said: “I will kill him instantly.”

He spread the sheet of leather and strewed sand upon it. Demons would fly before his insanity.

A youth went, swiftly as the wind, to the old man and said: “The king has accused thee of a crime.”

The old man made his ablutions, put on a shroud, went to the king, and began to speak.

The cunning king wrung his hands and turned away his face in malice.

He said: “I hear that thou hast spoken, calling me revengeful and a murderer.

“Thou art aware of my wise rule. Why dost thou call me a tyrannical demon?”

The old man replied: “I am not asleep; I have called thee worse things than this.

“Youth and age are endangered by thy actions; towns and villages are afflicted by thy intrigues.

“I who have thus recounted thy faults, bear the mirror reflecting thy virtues and vices.

“Since the mirror shows thy picture correctly, break thyself; it is an error to break the mirror.

“See that I speak truly and heed me; if it be not so, kill me on the scaffold.”

When the sage admitted the truth, his veracity affected the heart of the king.

When the king recognised his superiority in truth, he realised the sage’s rectitude and his own falsehood.

He said: “Take away the funeral herbs and the shroud; dress him in our robe of honour and perfume him with ambergris.”

He turned away from tyranny, practised justice and the care of his subjects.

None ever hid his own veracity, and no one suffered loss on account of truth.

Be truthful, that thou mayest gain salvation; if thou art truthful, God will give thee victory.

If true words are all pearls, they are indeed bitter, because “Truth is bitter.”

When thy words are true, God is thy helper.

The nature and the heart of Nezāmi are both true, and because of this truth, his affairs are in order.
