Oh thou, who art entirely happy, eating fodder like a donkey and a cow,

Inattentive to this centre round which the sun revolves, negligent of this circle of lapis lazuli,

The matter is for the enlightened ones. What grief have the enlightened for the world?

Come to thy work. Why art thou sleeping? Do the work which has been allotted to thee.

Why art thou in a drunken sleep? They are in ambush; the experienced do not act thus.

See this mound of grief, look forward, observe carefully, and see thine own incapacity.

Thy reason is forgetful like an old man; that he may remember thee, remind him.

If thou hadst not the honour of reason, who would call upon thy name? Who would worship thee?

Reason is Messiah, do not rebel against it. If thou art not an ass, do not lead the ass into the mire.

Either get thy light from the path of reason, or keep thy skirt far from its door.

Do not intoxicate the reason which educates. Do not make the eagle a bait for the sparrow.

The hostility of thy reason made the wine which is lawful everywhere, unlawful.

What is wine that thy honour should be in its bowl? Reason is that spring which they call wine.

Although wine takes away the grief of the world, oh master, do not take that which takes away reason.

Wine is salt mixed with the heart, poured upon the heart of the saltless ones.

If thou didst desire advice, do not take that which makes thee unmindful of all things.

How ignorant is the man who tastes that which draws the line of oblivion across him.

Blind the eye of vain thoughts; fetter the foot of drunkenness.

Oh thou, who, like an Alef, art in love with thine own stature, thou lovest the gloom of thine own melancholy.

If thou art an Alef, be a bird without wings; otherwise be like bā, a letter with a drooping head.

When thou art, like an Alef, the ornament of the assembly, thou hast nothing and art poor like an Alef.

Thou art not a thorn, that thou desirest height; it is better for thee to be humble like the rose.

Thou art not a child, do not play games. Thou art not life, do not aspire to length.

The day has come to an end, and the sun has set. The darkness grows, as the light decreases.

I have heard that, when the day draws towards evening, the shadows become twice as long.

Why dost thou, like a garden, worship shade? Be the destroyer of darkness, like the light of a lamp.

If thou canst remove thy shadow, thy faults will become invisible like thy shadow.

Not everyone can sit in darkness; only the spring of the water of life is in darkness.

Oh thou, whose head and feet are upside down, thy judgment is in confusion.

Dawn gives thee the golden bowl, that thou mayest cleanse thyself from thyself.

When thou washest thy garments in this basin, draw thy water from the source of the sun.

The disc of the sun, which is thy soap, cleanses thy blood-stained garments.

Nature has kindled such a fire in thee, that no water remained in the heart of thy life.

If thy body is not cleansed from the stain of selfishness, yet gold which is not all red is still gold.

If we say all creation is pure, how then should the belly of hell be filled?

A man gets the armour of truthfulness from fire alone.

Falsehood will lead thee to need and poverty; thou wilt free thyself from all sorrows, if thou art truthful.

If, like the scales, thou art true, entrust the truth of thy heart to the balance.

Every barley-corn and every grain which thy hand takes out of thy measure and thy scales,

Is each one in its own place, and on the day of retribution they will be brought forward.

They will disclose thy secret; they will show thy short measure and thine overcharges.

Do not thyself hold the balance; if thou holdest it, give more and take less.

The rose embraced the thorn from its crookedness; the sugar cane got its sweetness from its uprightness.

Where truth raises its banner, the help of God clasps it by the hand.
