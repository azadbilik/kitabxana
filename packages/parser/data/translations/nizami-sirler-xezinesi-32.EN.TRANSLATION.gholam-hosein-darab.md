Oh thou, who on earth are beloved like the heaven, thy lovers are both earth and heaven.

Thy work, since thou hadst knowledge, was greater than thou couldst imagine.

At the beginning, thou didst not take milk but sugar from that nurse who brought thee up.

Thou shouldst have greater beauty. What beauty is greater than this?

Because of the brush which they used, they have made of thee a beautiful picture.

They have tied the cord of life to thy heart, and they have girt thy waist with the jewel of the body.

It is better for thee to be weak, because in this meadow a fat gazelle cannot keep pace with a lean one.

The living creatures which are thy slaves, are the birds in thy snare.

Since thou art a Phœnix, be the glory of the work; eat little, say little, and harm none.

Whatever thou mayest see in creation, is working in this workshop.

The owl which is known as the bird of ill-omen, is the nightingale of the treasure of the ruin.

Whatever exists on this curtain, is worthy of a body and has a value for its life.

Though they have fewer gems from thy sea than thou, like thee they are the jewels of this world.

When thou bringest into account the idea of less and more, expect to pay damage according to the injury.

The good and the evil in this world are for thy use; in good and in evil they hold up a mirror to thee.

If thou shouldst give shoes, they will give thee a hat. If thou exposest the secrets of others, they will expose thy secrets as does the moon.

Arise, do not, like the dawn, divulge the secrets of others, so that the keeper of thy secrets may be like the night.

The veil of the bee is the petal of the rose, and thine is this lace curtain of heaven.

How long wilt thou fly for food like a fly into this spider’s web?

The veiled ones who own the world, hid thy secret behind the curtain.

Thou didst come out from behind that curtain, therefore thy secrets were unveiled.

Do not even bid farewell to the heart which is outside the veil; lend no ear to that which is not behind the veil.

The actor who is behind this curtain, did not tie this veil on thy head without reason.

Do not hold to anything but this curtain. Do not play any melody except this tune.

Listen to this melody and awake; be entrusted with the mysteries behind the veil.

Thou couldst make thy body purer than the soul, if thou wouldst imprison it for forty days.

Man is honoured by imprisonment; for this reason Joseph lay in prison.

One cannot find the value of the heart and the dignity of the soul except by discipline.

Entrust the silver of nature to discipline; produce the gold of nature by discipline,

So that through discipline thou mayest reach a state where thou wilt be drawn from humiliation to glory.

When thou hast broken in the wild horse of nature, the coin of sincerity will be minted in thy name.

When reason and nature become thy friends, the story of the ironsmith and the perfumer is true.

One will blister thy face with heat, and one will perfume thee with the scent of ambergris.

The baggage of nature holds no salvation. In the cage of the bird there is little life.

That which is contrary to habit, is the leader of the caravan of happiness.

To turn away from lust is sovereignty; to abandon it altogether is prophetic power.

If for an instant desire is under thy control, then bring the shoes, because paradise is thine.

Jangle the bells of lust; be the servant of faith, not the hireling of the devil.

Fly for safety to the sanctuary of religion, that thou mayest be free of care on the day of resurrection.

The perfume of the Prophet is the guardian of Bu Tāleb from the raging fire of hell.

Truly, the benevolence of the blessed is the protecting armour of the saints.
