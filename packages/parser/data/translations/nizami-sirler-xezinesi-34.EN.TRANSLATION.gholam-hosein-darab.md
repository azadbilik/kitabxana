Before the forerunners of existence had drunk water from the sea of munificence,

There was no right or left hand on this kingdom. There was no dust on the road of this earth.

The promise of delay had not come to its end. No manifestation had come out from behind the curtain.

Night and day had no sign of humility; the soul and the body were not combined for existence.

The elements still struggled together; the laws of balance were not yet manifest.

The grace of generosity showed benevolence and let one drop fall from its sea.

By that one drop, this blue heaven immediately began to revolve.

They raised dust from the running water; they produced thine essence from that accident.

When thou arisest from this work-shop, it will be as dust arising from the road.

How beautiful was that moment when thou wast not; thy picture was unpainted, and thy soul existed without thee!

The eye of heaven was free from this search; the ear of the earth was free from these discussions.

So long as thou didst not tread this road, existence was full of gratitude to non-existence.

Night and day were free from the trouble of thy pregnancy. Vegetation was unfertile, and nature was mature.

The garden of the world was not troubled by the thorn; the earth did not suffer disturbing dust.

The fortune of the Gemini who had girded their loins, was free from the opening of thy vein.

When the moon was eclipsed, it was not disgraced in the earth by the beating of thy tray.

Venus had not yet been dishonoured on this clay, and the pinions of the angel Hārut had not yet been shed in Babylon.

Earth and heaven were without thee. Thou wast absent, but the care for thee existed.

When the decree of the universe was renewed through thee, the turquoise dome resounded with thy fame.

The star did not escape thy evil eye. The pageantry of the cradle of the stars was broken.

The month and the year were without movement, until thou knewest their secret.

The face of the world which was a clear mirror, was darkened by these breaths.

Thou tookest the torch of dawn to the evening and didst call it false and true.

May the dust of the earth be in the mouth of heaven, because it has girt its loins in thy service.

In heaven they call thee the fruit of the soul. Listen, because these are spoken words.

Alas, that thy crown is better than thy head, the covering better than the dog, the nosebag better than the ass.

There was much boasting in this world, that the world is not worth to thee so much as a grain of barley on the dust of the road.

Thou wouldst exchange a soul for a handful of dust; thou givest a whole world for a particle of clay.

Oh thou, by whom the face of the earth is troubled, it is better that like a treasure thy place should be under the earth.

The oil of thy brain which is like quicksilver, is frozen by this world.

So that it may not confine thee as in a hazel nut, abandon this world.

Day and night are not ermine and sable; this dappled stoat is really a dragon.

Thou art not a cat; do not stretch out thy hand. Do not dally with the worthless fickle woman.

The lion has slavered on this road; why dost thou bend down thy head to the water like a stag?

If heaven shows thee water, do not be deceived; it is a mirage.

Do not gallop swiftly, thinking thou hast seen the water of heaven. Drink the water of thy mouth, because what thou hast seen is only salt.

Make a plan, that thou mayest not be thirsty. Be like tabāshir, which is obtained by burning the tabāshir canes.

So long as thy Joseph was out of the pit, his gaze was on divine Egypt.

Thou earnest from the blue heaven with yellow cheeks, when thou didst fall into this pit.

The vinegar of thy eyebrow was not a remedy for all thy bile and for thy yellow face.

Since thy fat had grown for a hundred years; how could it be cured by the vinegar of thy eyebrow, which is but ten years old?

Thou hast seen the blood of thy father on these seven battlefields. Do not dishonour thyself for these seven loaves.

Thou art setting fire to thine own harvest; thou art striking blows at thine own fortune.

Gallop and urge on thy steed, because the arena is thine. Give orders, because the command is thine.

During these few days of life in which thou holdest the cup, eat well, sleep well, and rest well.

Truly, it was an act of great tyranny to set thee free from that rope so lightly.

Thy feet are fettered, and thy back is bent; yet thou art still frying in thine own fat;

Therefore thou art here the fuel of the kitchen fire; on the day of resurrection thou wilt be the brushwood burnt in hell.

Even supposing that this belly of thine is full of water and of bread, oh fool, thou wilt then be worth no more.

If a man could lengthen his life through eating more, then he who eats most would live longest.

Life is short, therefore it is precious. The value of life lies in its brevity.

Eat little and see what great ease thou wilt have; eat much and see the great discomfort.

What has thy reason to do with thy food? Only thy greed sets thee to this work.

Thy greed is impatient to do mischief. Abandon this fool who deceives the intelligent.

As opposed to thy greed, they have given thee reason, that thou mayest not eat what is not given thee.

I fear that this work to which greed puts thee, will turn thee to its own colour.

Every good and every evil in this world receives colour from the other.
